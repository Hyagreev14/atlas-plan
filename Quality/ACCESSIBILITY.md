# Accessibility and usability requirements

## Keyboard
Primary workflows must be keyboard reachable. Define logical tab order and visible focus. Shortcuts must be discoverable and avoid OS conflicts. Menus and dialogs must behave predictably.

## Visual clarity
Aim for WCAG AA contrast where applicable. Do not rely on colour alone. Support OS scaling and readable text. Keep focus visible in both themes and avoid tiny controls.

## Motion
Motion communicates state, not decoration. Provide reduced-motion behaviour in implementation. Avoid flashing, looping decoration and transitions that delay tasks.

## Assistive technology
Use meaningful labels, roles and state announcements for controls, navigation, dialogs, lists and dynamic feedback. Icon-only controls require accessible names.

## Usability review
Ask reviewers to complete key journeys without coaching. Record hesitation, confusing labels, lost context and recovery problems; turn repeated friction into design changes.
